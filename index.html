1.1.3 Professional Services

  • Services sales: no physical inventory delivered; instead consulting, support, or maintenance.
  • Configured via Project System, connected to SD for an end-to-end cycle.
  • Example: S4 Technologies signs 1-year consulting contract with SAP (data migration project, two consultants on-site Philadelphia → Indiana, hotel two weeks). End-to-end steps:
  • Presales documents (inquiry, quotation, contract).
  • Sales order with total contracted hours.
  • Hourly rates via condition records.
  • Time sheet entry (two weeks on-site).
  • Expenses (airfare, hotels, meals).
  • RRB document (SD → Project Systems integration) for hours and expenses.
  • Billing document for hours and expenses.
  • Revenue postings for hours and expenses.
  • Key difference vs product sales: services are transacted on an as-incurred basis; billing/revenue values are dynamic (rate depends on on-site vs off-site; expenses known only when incurred).
  • Project System and resource-related billing (RRB) detailed in Chapter 7.

1.2 Sales and Finance

  • Sales has finance impact at nearly every subprocess; almost no sales process is complete without an underlying finance transaction.
  • Finance manages/monitors general ledger numbers: accounts receivable (sales) or accounts payable (procurement).
  • Common integration points: pricing and taxation, account determination, credit management, profitability analysis, intercompany billing.

[Figure 1.6

: Sales and Finance]

1.2.1 Pricing and Taxation

  • Pricing = determining prices (CRM or S/4HANA via condition records).
  • Example: laptop bundle invoiced at USD 500; internal components laptop USD 300, software USD 100, peripherals USD 100; cost USD 450, profit USD 50.
  • Tax = charging tax based on location and product/service type; federal/geographic rules; can use third-party tax engines.
  • Configured via pricing procedure, condition technique, access sequence, condition tables.
  • Key transactions:
  • VOK0: SD Pricing Configuration.
  • VK11: Condition Record Creation.
  • OVK1: Tax Category Determination by Country.
  • FTXP: Tax Code Configuration.

1.2.2 Account Determination

  • Determines GL accounts for various SD/finance transactions; standard SAP process.
  • Key transaction VKOA — sets up GL determination for revenue, unbilled, deferred, trade AR (depends on access sequence).
  • Pricing procedures trigger pricing conditions inside sales orders; each condition has an account key linking SD to finance.
  • Condition types include: price, discount/surcharge, expense reimbursements, tax, extra pay, fees/differential, tax classification, sales deal determination, compare price protection, totals record for fees, wage withholding tax.
  • Example (10% discount on bundle): price USD 500, discount USD 50 (10%), tax USD 25 (5%).
  • Other key transactions:
  • OB40: Accounting Maintain Configuration.
  • FS00: General Ledger Master Configuration.

1.2.3 Credit Management

  • Offers credit limit and payment terms per company credit policy; common in B2B.
  • Factors: risk assessment/category, risk class, risk score, credit evaluation, payment history.
  • Config: credit control area, credit segment, risk score rules, risk class/category, credit limits.
  • Example: SAP configures credit management for S4 Technology (risk score, class, category, segment, control area); on-time payments auto-increase credit limit and/or extend payment terms.
  • Key transactions:
  • OB45: Credit Control Area definition.
  • OVA8: Automatic Credit Control definition.
  • OVAK: Sales Document Types – Credit Limit Check.
  • OVAD: Delivery Types – Credit Limit Check.

1.2.4 Profitability Analysis

  • Evaluates and measures profits; grow profits by expanding profitable trends/strategies.
  • Two profit drivers: earnings and revenue; contributing factors: customers, vendors, products.
  • Profitability determined by characteristics (sales/customer groups, materials/material groups, countries, regions, states).
  • P&L run at company-code level (finance) or for organizational units (countries, regions, product lines, customers).

Formula — P&L: Profit & loss = Revenue − Expenses − Taxes Applied (laptop bundle): P&L = 525 − 450 − 25 = 50

  • Key transactions:
  • KEA0: Maintain Operating Concern.
  • KEA0: Maintain Characteristics.

1.2.5 Intercompany Billing

  • Business transactions between different company codes under the same parent company.
  • Example: S4 Tech India (APAC) and S4 Tech USA (Americas); UAE order fulfilled by S4 Tech USA stock; intercompany sales/billing recorded between entities; nets to zero at parent S4 Technology GL level while each records own P&L.
  • Key transactions:
  • VF01: Create Billing Document.
  • VFX3: Release Billing Document to Accounting.
  • VF21: Invoice List.
  • VTFL: Copy Control Delivery to Billing.

1.3 Sales and Supply Chain Management

  • SCM viewed as two subprocesses: materials management and production planning.
  • Materials management forms: availability check, STO, GL account determination, third-party processing, inventory management.
  • Production planning forms: make to order (MTO), material requirements planning (MRP), variant configuration, transfer of requirements.

[Figure 1.7

: Sales and Supply Chain Management]

1.3.1 Materials Management

  • Components: availability check, STO, GL account determination, third-party process, inventory management.

Availability Check

  • Synonymous with available-to-promise (ATP); enhanced as SAP S/4HANA for advanced ATP.
  • Balances demand, supply, and available stock to avoid excess storage.

Formula — ATP quantity = Quantity in hand + Supply − Demand Example: 500 + 200 − 800 = −100 → promise 700 laptops now, source deficit 100 (vendor order, STO, or third-party).

  • Key transactions:
  • OVZ9: Scope of Availability Check.
  • OVZ2: Availability Check Group Configuration.
  • OPPQ: Purchasing Processing Time.
  • OVLK: Storage Location Determination.

Stock Transport Order

  • STO = moving inventory between plants (same company code = intracompany STO; different company codes = intercompany STO).
  • Example: HP moves laptop from Mumbai plant to Bangalore plant, then ships to customer.
  • Key transactions:
  • VL10B: Purchase Orders for Execution of Replenishment Delivery.
  • OVKK: Pricing Procedure Determination.
  • MIGO: Goods Receipt.
  • MIRO: Accounts Payable Invoice.

General Ledger Account Determination

  • Determines GL accounts for SD/materials management transactions with finance impact; transaction key connects rule to GL.
  • Example: PGI document with two line items (laptops → inventory GL, freight → freight GL) via Transaction OBYC.
  • Other key transactions:
  • MR21: Material Price Update.
  • OB62: Company Code to Chart of Accounts Assignment.
  • OMSK: Account Category Reference and Valuation Class.
  • OMWN: Account Grouping Configuration.

Third-Party Processing

  • Also drop shipping; seller sources from external vendor, no physical inventory held; vendor ships directly to customer; price difference = seller revenue.
  • Example: customer pays S4 Technology USD 2,000 for SAP license; SAP bills S4 Technology USD 1,600; profit USD 400.
  • Key transactions:
  • VL10: Delivery Due List.
  • ME21N: Purchase Order Creation.
  • MIGO: Goods Receipt.
  • MIRO: Vendor Invoice.

Inventory Management

  • Manages inventory through the warehouse; each movement has a movement type.
  • Stages (per stock type): document concept, goods receipt, goods issue, stock transfer.
  • Example: S4 Technology fulfillment via third-party/ATP or STO inventory management.
  • Key transactions:
  • MMBE: Stock Overview.
  • MD04: Stocks/Requirements List.
  • MB11: Goods Movement.
  • OBYC: GL Account Determination for Inventory Management.

1.3.2 Production Planning

  • Components: MTO, MRP, variant configuration, transfer of requirements.

Make to Order

  • Manufacture only after customer order; advantages: no excess limbo stock; products designed to unique customer requirements.
  • Start point = sales order; if raw materials in stock → manufacture; else purchase order → procure → build → deliver → bill.
  • Example: assembled desktop (Windows 10 Pro, Intel Core i7, 16 GB RAM, 1 TB HDD, 24-inch HD monitor, wireless keyboard/mouse); some in stock, some ordered; production order + purchase order → goods receipt → assemble → delivery → bill.
  • Key transactions:
  • MD11: Planned Order.
  • CO05N: Release Production Order.
  • VA01: Customer Sales Order.
  • VF01: Invoicing.

Material Requirements Planning

  • Strategy (mostly automated) for efficient SCM; uses budgeting/forecasting to estimate production time/quantity, avoiding overstocking or delays; reduces manual errors.
  • Controlled by material master master data settings and node configuration via Transaction SPRO.
  • Example: order 500 laptops (delivery June 1); 100 ready stock + 200 from Dell in May → shortfall 200 to manufacture or order.

Formula — Available stock = Plant stock − Safety stock + Receipt (purchase order, firmed purchase order) − Requirement quantity (forecast requirements)

  • Key transactions:
  • MD61: Create Planned Independent Requirements.
  • MD02: MRP Single-Item, Multi-Level.
  • MP30: Execute Forecast Run.
  • MD04: Stocks/Requirements List.

Variant Configuration

  • Materials configured as dynamic/variable, determined on sales order per ad hoc customer requirements (type, dimension, quantity, etc.); SD queries material master for only the relevant portion + characteristics.
  • Example: assembled desktop where "components" is a variable → final priced order built from selected components.
  • Key setup components: material type, material characteristics, material class, configuration profile, variant condition type on pricing procedure, pricing condition records.
  • Key transactions:
  • CT04: Creation of Characteristics.
  • CL01: Creation of Class.
  • CU41: Creation of Configuration Profile.

Transfer of Requirements

  • SD transfers requirements (quantity, delivery date) to MRP when a sales order is created; MRP ensures material availability on the date.
  • Key components: requirement class, requirement type, schedule line category.
  • Example: order 100 laptops (delivery Aug 22); on save, transfer of requirements sends quantity + date to MRP; MRP holds stock or triggers vendor order.
  • Key transactions:
  • MD01: MRP Run.
  • OVZH: Requirement Type.
  • OVZG: Requirement Class.

4.1 Available-to-Promise

  • ATP matches supply to demand so customers never face stock non-availability while minimizing warehouse space.

4.1.1 What Is Available-to-Promise?

  • ATP lets companies store the bare minimum of products, balancing client satisfaction and profitability.
  • "ATP quantities" = quantity a company can commit to selling in the near future.
  • [Formula] ATP quantity = Quantity in hand + Supply – Demand
  • Quantity in hand: total stock available in the organization.
  • Supply: planned orders, scheduled receipts (POs), purchase requisitions.
  • Demand: products customers are ready to buy or for which production orders exist (sales orders, component demands).
  • Remediation when ATP is unavailable: check ATP on another plant + replenish via STO; use backorder processing to redirect stock to higher-priority customer; substitute similar material; system uses backward scheduling to create a future-date schedule line.
  • SAP S/4HANA for advanced ATP — separate license required.

[Figure 4.1

: Advanced ATP Functionalities]

  • Business benefits: release for delivery, product allocation check, alternative-based confirmation (ABC); manages allocations in shortages; changes confirmations; substitutes delivering plant via ABC.
  • Capabilities of each functionality:
  • Product availability check: key SD functionality; supports STOs, special stocks (consignment), batch management; faster due to SAP HANA.
  • Backorder processing: adjust commitments / override stock blockages via Fiori app using win, gain, redistribute, fill, lose strategies; ABC available since release 1909.
  • Product allocations: distribute supply fairly when demand exceeds supply; define allocation procedure and assign to products/plants.
  • Alternative-based confirmation (ABC): auto-selects alternative locations/items; location substitution handled by rule-based ATP in SAP APO.
  • Release for delivery: manual backorder processing via Release for Delivery app.

Process Overview

[Figure 4.2

: Process Overview of Availability Check]

  • Triggered when sales order entered manually or via EDI/IDocs; check is always plant + material combination. Plant determined via customer-material info record, customer master, or material master.
  • Checking group (material master + Customizing) decides whether check runs; check also possible via Transaction CO09.
  • Delivery scheduling calculates material availability date from requested delivery date.
  • [Formula] Material availability date = Requested delivery date – Transportation time – Loading time – Pick/pack time
  • Requested delivery date: customer-requested date.
  • Transportation time: transportation lead time + transit time.
  • Loading time: time to load products for dispatch.
  • Pick/pack time: days to allocate + pick and pack products.
  • [Formula] ATP quantity = Quantity in hand + Supply – Demand (generalized formula for ATP)
  • [Formula – SAP elaborated] ATP quantity = Quantity in hand for the plant + Supply (Purchase requisition + Purchase order + Planned order) – Demand (Sales order + Delivery + Reservations)
  • Logic set in scope of availability check (Transaction OVZ9).
  • Orders confirmed if ATP quantity > requested quantity; past availability date → forward scheduling (creates two schedule lines); RLT activation confirms quantities despite non-availability.

4.3 General Ledger Account Determination in Deliveries

  • Material valuation = monetary value of inventory at period end; drives COGS and profitability.
  • In S/4HANA, valuation configured at material-type level: valuated vs nonvaluated vs nonstock.
  • UNBW (nonvaluated: quantity only, e.g., office stationery).
  • NLAG (nonstock: neither stock nor valuation; one-time purchases).
  • Price control: S (standard) or V (moving average); defaulted at material type or set at material master.
  • Standard price (S): value always at standard; variance to price difference account; update via Transaction MR21.
  • [Formula] Total value = Standard price (per base unit of measure) × Total stock
  • Moving average price (V): price auto-adjusted on variances; difference posted to stock account.
  • [Formula] Moving average price = Total stock value ÷ Total stock quantity
  • Account determination configured in Transaction OBYC.

6 Revenue Recognition

  • Revenue recognition: businesses calculate and post revenues to the G/L following a revenue recognition model; can be time-based or event-based.
  • Chapter covers: revenue recognition concepts + IFRS 15, SAP Revenue Accounting and Reporting (RAR) configuration through SD integration, Transaction NWBC, BRFplus, and optimized contract management (OCM).

6.1 What Is Revenue Recognition?

  • [Formula] Revenue = (Number of products sold) × (Cost per product) (high-level).

6.1.1 IFRS 15 Compliance

  • IFRS 15 governs how contractual revenues are recognized/accounted/reported; universal across industries. SAP launched RAR in 2015 to support it, built on a five-step model.

[Figure 6.1

: IFRS 15 Five-Step Model] — five steps:

  • Identify the customer contract (products/services, payment terms, price). Example: SAP sells disclosure bundle to S4 Foods at USD 2,200 (license + 12-month maintenance), 30-day terms.
  • Identify the individual performance obligations (POBs) (line items = License POB, Maintenance POB).
  • Determine the transaction price (total contracted price USD 2,200; SSP = standalone selling price: license USD 1,500, maintenance USD 1,700).
  • Allocate the price (core RAR differentiator): License POB allocated USD 1,031.25; Maintenance POB USD 1,168.75.
  • [Formula] Allocated price = SSP percentage × Total transaction price, where SSP percentage = (Individual SSP ÷ Total SSP) × 100
  • Recognize the revenue (fulfillment triggers): License = event-based; Maintenance = time-based.

7 Resource-Related Billing

  • RRB is a prime integration point between SD and Project System; key for service-based industries.
  • Two types in standard S/4HANA: Project System RRB and customer service RRB; chapter focuses on Project System RRB (more widely used, synchronizes with SD).

  • Three functionalities feed DIP: Project System, finance/controlling, materials management. SD accepts processed DIP data, combines with billing rates to create a billing request → invoiced.
  • RRB is called the SD–Project System integration point because cost elements (FI/CO) + master data (MM) are attached to a WBS in DIP before going to SD; the WBS (a Project System component) drives the process.

7.1 Project-Based Billing

  • RRB = billing a customer based on resources consumed by a project stakeholder (hours + expenses, as-incurred, not fixed prices).

7.1.1 Project System

8 Production Planning

  • Production planning applies where tangible goods are sold to end customers; sales document requirements transfer to production planning via configuration elements
  • Focus: intra-ERP integration between Sales and Distribution (SD) and Production Planning (PP) in SAP S/4HANA, giving a 360° view of order-to-cash and plan-to-produce cycles
  • Chapter covers: MRP process, transfer of requirements, variant configuration, make-to-order (MTO) process and planning strategies

8.1 Material Requirements Planning

  • MRP = inventory management system to increase business productivity by estimating raw material quantities and scheduling timely deliveries
  • Purpose: increase inventory efficiency, ensure proper inventory available for production at the right time and lowest cost
  • Historical background:
  • First conceptualized/implemented via computers by aerospace industry (General Electric, Rolls Royce), early 1950s
  • Reinvented to work with the existing Polaris program
  • Stanley Black & Decker pioneered MRP use in 1964
  • Over 700 businesses adopted MRP by 1975; continuously updated since
  • Benefits: improves efficiency, flexibility, profitability; increases worker productivity; improves product quality; reduces material/labor costs; avoids production delays and stock outs
  • MRP converts sales-generated requirements into procurement proposals (planned orders, production orders)

8.1.1 Process Flow

  • Prerequisite: filled material master data relevant to MRP (master data covered in Chapter 3, Section 3.2)
  • [Figure 8.1

    : In-House Production Process]
  • Process steps:
  • Starts with forecasting data preparation via sales and operations planning to determine production quantities
  • Sales planning (demand planning) addresses future needs without considering stocks or capacity; based on historical sales figures
  • Results used to plan production quantities, factoring in initial stocks and capacities
  • Requirements forecasted from past sales/consumption data = planned independent requirements, fed into system via demand management (Transaction MD61)
  • Planned independent requirements + customer requirement (from sales order) fed into the demand program
  • System creates procurement proposal after running MRP (Transaction MD02):
  • Purchase requisition → purchase order → procure-to-pay cycle
  • Planned order → production order → plan-to-produce cycle
  • Inventory management manages quantities made available by production or external procurement
  • Note: For in-house production materials (Procurement Type = In-House Production in MRP 2 view), maintain Work Center and Routing Data fields in material master to determine production dates

8.1.2 Implementation Methods

  • Product planning differs by product type (raw materials, finished products, semifinished products, trading goods, etc.)
  • MRP type maintained in material master MRP 1 view (Chapter 3, Section 3.2.6); PP consultant selects MRP procedure during MRP type configuration
  • Menu path: Production • Material Requirements Planning • Master Data • Check MRP Types
  • [Figure 8.2

    : MRP Types]     — MRP Type PD has MRP Procedure D (Material Requirements Planning)
  • [Figure 8.3

    : MRP Procedure]     — MRP procedure categorized into MRP and Consumption-Based Planning (CBP); CBP further classified into reorder point planning, forecast-based planning, time-phased planning

Material Requirements Planning: Procedure D

  • Sales orders, planned independent requirements, reservations, dependent requirements (from BOM explosion) all directly planned as requirements
  • Procurement proposal generated only if requirements result in stock shortage at a specific time
  • Especially useful for finished products, critical assemblies and components
  • For in-house manufactured materials, system calculates dependent requirements by exploding the BOM; planned orders created at each BOM level to cover shortages
  • Net requirements calculation performed after planning file check, at plant level
  • Available stock formula:

    Available stock = Quantity in hand for the plant − Safety stock + Receipt (Purchase requisition + Purchase order + Planned order) − Demand (Sales order + Delivery + Reservations + Planned independent and customer requirement)

Consumption-Based Planning

  • CBP uses forecasts/statistical procedures to determine future requirements based on past consumption values
  • Planned independent/dependent requirements not taken into account in net requirements calculation
  • Triggered when stock falls below predefined reorder point OR by forecast requirements from past consumption
  • Simple procedures for areas with no in-house production/manufacturing
  • Three implementation methods:
Reorder point planning
  • SAP S/4HANA checks whether available stocks are below reorder point; if below, generates procurement proposal
  • MRP type classification:
  • VB: Manual reorder point planning (reorder point determined manually)
  • VM: Automatic reorder point planning (reorder point calculated automatically via material forecast)
  • Reorder point should cover average material requirement/consumption during replenishment lead time (procurement processing time + planned delivery time + goods receipt processing time)
  • Safety stock consideration: covers excess consumption within replenishment lead time and requirements from delivery delays; included in reorder level
  • Values critical for defining safety stock:
  • Previous consumption values or future requirements
  • Timeliness for vendor/production delivery
  • Service level to be achieved
  • Forecast error (deviation from expected)
  • Net requirements calculation in reorder point planning: carried out only once stock falls below reorder level; excludes customer requirement, planned independent requirement, reservation

    Available warehouse stock = Plant stock + Open order quantity (Purchase order, Firmed planned order, Firmed purchase requisition)
Forecast-based planning
  • Historical data used in material forecast to estimate future requirements (forecast requirements)
  • Forecast requirements immediately available via Transaction MD04; performed at regular intervals; auto-adjusts to current consumption
  • Parameters kept in material master forecasting view; consumption data in Additional Data tab
  • Run forecast from Transaction MP30 or material master
  • MRP type = VV
  • Net requirements calculation formula:

    Available stock = Plant stock − Safety stock + Receipt (Purchase order, Firmed purchase order) − Requirement quantity (Forecast requirements)
Time-phased planning
  • Historical data used in material forecast; planning run performed only at predefined intervals (specific dates during the week)
  • Useful when vendor consistently delivers on a particular day
  • Transaction MM02 to enter MRP type R1 or R2 and the planning cycle in material master

8.1.3 Scheduling

  • MRP calculates/plans procurement quantities and dates after net requirements calculation
  • Procurement Type field (MRP 2 view) determines in-house / external / both
  • Procurement proposals scheduled: external → delivery and release dates; in-house → production date; BOM explosion determines dependent requirements
  • Procurement proposals after MRP:
  • Planned orders → externally procured or in-house produced materials
  • Purchase requisitions → externally procured materials
  • Schedule lines → externally procured materials with vendor source list entry and scheduling agreement
  • Purchase requisitions and planned orders are internal elements (changeable, reschedulable, deletable anytime)
  • Date calculation:
  • In-house production:
  • System always generates planned orders (forecast production volumes); converted to production orders when MRP controller is satisfied
  • Basic dates (order start date, order finish date) used for scheduling
  • System calculates basic dates each planning run; always uses backward scheduling; switches to forward scheduling if start date is in the past
  • External procurement:
  • MRP procedure D (Material Requirements Planning) or S (Forecast-Based Planning) → backward scheduling
  • MRP procedure B (Reorder Point Planning) → forward scheduling
  • Planned orders → order start and finish dates; purchase requisitions → delivery date and order release date
  • Note: Backward/forward scheduling explained in Chapter 4, Section 4.1.3
  • If both in-house and external procurement defined (indicator X in Procurement Type):
  • Overwrite indicator in material master
  • Convert planned order into production order or purchase requisition
  • Use quota arrangements to split percentages between in-house and external

8.4 Make to Order

  • MTO = manufacturing a product specifically for a customer only when order entered into system; stock maintained with respect to sales order
  • Primary benefits: track sales order progress; flexibility to offer products meeting specific requirements; waste reduction; avoids overstocking
  • Heavy integration between PP and SD via requirement type and requirement class

8.4.1 Planning Strategies

  • Material categorized by demand patterns; planning strategies represent business procedures for material planning and production
  • Usually associated with finished item; can also be planning material or assembly material; assigned on material master MRP 2 screen
  • Strategies define: how forecasted independent requirements interact with customer requirements; whether availability check permitted; where production costs are settled
  • System determines correct requirement type when creating planned independent requirements or sales orders by assigning planning strategy
  • Recommended: use SAP-provided strategies; if no match, copy existing strategy and modify (don't change as-delivered strategies)
  • Common material planning strategies:
  • Make to stock (MTS)
  • Materials manufactured regardless of customer requirements; production dependent on independent planning from demand management; sales orders don't affect production
  • Product stocked then delivered as needed; appropriate for forecastable finished goods
  • Two strategies: 10 (net requirements planning, takes stock into account) and 11 (gross requirements planning, ignores stock; planned quantities produced even if stock available)
  • Make to order (MTO)
  • Production started after sales order requirement passed to system; production orders directly linked to sales orders; no forecast/planned independent requirements needed
  • Materials segregated and uniquely assigned to specific sales orders; planned for production using sales order number
  • Material usually complex to manufacture; accurate forecast not achievable; customers willing to wait lead time
  • Common strategies: 20 and 21; difference = cost settlement to sales order
  • Assemble to order (ATO)
  • Falls under MTO; combines benefits of MTO and MTS; fast delivery while allowing product adaptation
  • Assembly time/cost minimal; component build time/cost can be significant
  • Generates production order to assemble finished product after sales order created
  • Example: ice cream parlor assembling flavors into a cone from available flavors
  • Configure to order (CTO)
  • Underpins MTO; design chosen after order received; uses customer requirements from predefined set of product features
  • Specific predefined variant created from combination of parts/assemblies; achieved via variant configuration functionality
  • Engineer to order (ETO)
  • Product designed and manufactured after order received; meets exact customer specifications
  • Ideal for highly configurable products needing close customer engagement; achieved via Project System and work breakdown structure (WBS)

A Electronic Data Interchange

  • EDI = process by which systems electronically transfer data between each other; minimum involves two systems: sender system and receiver system
  • Sender and receiver can belong to same company or different companies
  • Examples:
  • SFDC (CRM) and SAP (ERP) within company ABC communicate via EDI
  • Company ABC supplies goods to company DEF; delivery documents transferred ABC → DEF via EDI
  • EDI integrations in SAP S/4HANA happen via: intermediate documents (IDocs), Simple Object Access Protocol (SOAP), application programming interfaces (APIs), SAP Business Network for Procurement (previously Ariba Network)
  • Appendix focus: EDI for SD using IDocs — IDoc structures, inbound/outbound roles, benefits, key IDoc types, EDI tables, EDI transactions
  • [Figure A.1: EDI Using IDocs] — shows generic EDI process (EDI-IDoc Inbound and EDI-IDoc Outbound) between sender and receiver system

A.1 Benefits of Electronic Data Interchange

  • Key benefits of using EDI:
  • Streamlined and efficient data exchange process
  • Fewer user errors and manual glitches
  • Cost savings
  • Sustainability (paper use reduced to almost zero)
  • Availability of electronic data for reporting
  • Easy options to troubleshoot and debug data interchange issues
  • Reduced timelines for data processing with quicker turnarounds and acknowledgements
  • Real-time data transmission

A.2 Electronic Data Interchange in Sales and Distribution

  • Traditionally, EDI in SD happened through IDocs
  • With SAP S/4HANA, SAP launched cloud-based solutions (SAP API Business Hub, SAP Business Network for Procurement) to support EDI; however, IDocs are still the preferred EDI option
  • IDocs work with SAP S/4HANA, SAP ERP, SAP R/3; translate data from one system to another; message types handling inbound and outbound transactions
  • Examples of SD EDI transactions: creating sales orders, sending invoices to customers, transferring post goods issue (PGI) documents, sharing contract purchase orders

A.2.1 IDocs

  • IDocs = standard SAP documents for connecting external systems to SAP or one SAP module to another; use a message interface to establish connections and transmit data
  • Native to SAP; controlled with standard transactions; customizable to business needs
  • Flexible: connect directly with sender/receiver systems or with intermediate ETL (extract, transform, load) tools such as Informatica
  • Data storage and data transmitting mediums with built-in logic that can be enhanced
  • Represented by an IDoc number; IDoc number ranges customizable; view IDocs via Transaction WE09
  • One-to-one mapping with business objects (one IDoc contains only one business object)
  • Example: IDoc type ORDERS01 could technically house message types QUOTES and ORDERSP, but at a given time contains only one, not both
  • IDoc structure = three record types: control record, data record, status record
  • [Figure A.2: IDoc Structure] — prototype showing one control record, many data records (segments) and status records; error-proof structure has only one control record and many data/status records

Control Record

  • Also denoted as EDI_DC; contains fields/data that control basic functioning of an IDoc
  • Info: IDoc number, direction, status, sender/receiver data, partner details, technical information, address data, additional details
  • Display via Transaction WE09 → enter IDoc number → Execute → click IDoc record → double-click Control Record entry
  • [Figure A.3: IDoc Control Record and the Typinfo Tab]
  • Control record has header section + details section with tabs: Typinfo, Partner, Techn.info, Address, Details
  • Header section (three fields):
  • IDoc number: unique number for an IDoc
  • Direction: 2 for Inbound, 1 for Outbound
  • Status: status of an IDoc; standard statuses provided; custom statuses possible (rarely needed)
  • Typinfo tab fields:
  • Basic type: IDoc type available/configured in SAP S/4HANA; standard basic types can be copied/customized (best practice: copy standard and add business logic); helps define IDoc structure
  • Extension: manually created IDoc type used with SAP standard basic IDoc type (e.g., basic type INTERNAL_ORDER with Extension of type On-Premise Internal Order / SaaS Internal Order)
  • IDoc Type: identifies IDoc types for SAP release 4.0 and prior; seldom used
  • Message Type: message type of an IDoc; core component of messages establishing connections between sender/receiver
  • Message Variant: unique user-defined variant for a message; controls which subprocess to run (e.g., INVOIC blank = new invoice, INVOIC variant MOD = modified invoice)
  • Message function: unique user-defined function code; controls subprocesses; if variant and function maintained in partner profile of receiving system, external sending system must also configure them for successful inbound transmission
  • Test Flag: checked to transfer IDocs to a test instance instead of productive instance
  • [Figure A.4: IDoc Control Record Partner Tab]
  • Partner tab — two sections with four fields each:
  • Recipient information:
  • Port: port name of recipient system
  • Partner Number: partner number of recipient system
  • Part. Type: partner type of recipient system (partner type + partner number make a system unique)
  • Function: partner function/role of recipient system (e.g., sold-to party, ship-to party)
  • Sender information:
  • Port: port name of sender system
  • Partner Number: partner number of sender system
  • Partn.Type: partner type of sender system
  • Partner Role: partner role/function of sender system (e.g., sold-to party, ship-to party)
  • Other three tabs:
  • Techn.info: technical info (SAP release number, output mode, serialization); control record creation and update times
  • Address: logical addresses and standard address management information
  • Details: interchange file, message group, reference, identification, EDI options (standard, version, message type)

Data Records

  • Also known as EDI_DD; host application data contained in an IDoc
  • Made of two core components: key section and segment
  • Key section: 55- or 63-byte component supporting the segment; describes structure/behavior of segment, helps with segment naming convention, assists in determining sequence and hierarchy of segments
  • Segments: store and process the data within a data record
  • Segments + key section referred to as SDATA (by ABAP developers); SDATA records = 1,000 bytes in length
  • One IDoc structure can have multiple data records (multiple SDATA records); hierarchy/chronology of data records depends on hierarchy/chronology of segments; SDATA retriggered per data record (different SDATA structure/rules possible per record)
  • View/modify via Transaction WE09 → enter IDoc number → Execute → click IDoc record → open Data records folder
  • [Figure A.5: IDoc Data Record Hierarchy] — data record hierarchy shown when clicking Data records folder
  • [Figure A.6: IDoc Data Record Details] — underlying technical details when double-clicking a data record entry
  • Example client: transfers data from Salesforce Apttus to SAP to create sales orders; separate segment record per data set (role data, partner data, item data each create separate segments)
  • Data record details section has two sections: Short Technical Information and Content of Selected Segment
  • Short Technical Information fields:
  • Direction: 1 outbound, 2 inbound (example is inbound → value 2)
  • Current Status: status of IDoc (status 53 = IDoc posted successfully)
  • Basic type: IDoc type configured in SAP (ZAPTTUS_ORDER1 = order IDoc type at example client)
  • Extension: extension IDoc type created by users; upward compatible only (blank at example client)
  • Message Type: message type (ZAPTTUS_ORDER = Z order message copied from standard ORDER)
  • Partner No.: partner number of sender system (APTTUS)
  • Partn.Type: partner type of sender system (LS = logical system)
  • Port: port name of sender system (TRFC)
  • Content of Selected Segment section: displays list of all fields in the selected segment transferred from sender (Apttus) to receiver (SAP)

Status Records

  • Also known as EDI_DS; display a series of statuses shown during an IDoc process
  • IDoc goes through many intermediary stages before it passes or fails posting; each stage tied to a status and status record
  • Example stages: IDoc added; IDoc ready to be passed to application; Application document posted
  • Status records = two characters in length; values range 01 to 41 for outbound IDocs, 50 to 73 for inbound IDocs
  • View/modify via Transaction WE09 → enter IDoc number → Execute → click IDoc record → open Status records folder
  • [Figure A.7: IDoc Status Record Hierarchy] — inbound IDoc example; went through four intermediate stages before posting successfully; statuses read bottom-up
  • Status stages walkthrough (inbound example):
  • Status 50 — IDoc added: IDoc successfully added to receiver system (first stage)
  • Status 64 — IDoc ready to be passed to application: passed initial validations, ready for receiver application (second stage)
  • Status 62 — IDoc passed to application: IDoc passed to receiver application (third stage)
  • Status 52 — Application document not fully posted: document created but not posted (e.g., sales order created but not saved/posted) (fourth stage)
  • Status 53 — Application document posted: IDoc processed successfully, application document posted without errors (e.g., sales order saved successfully in SD) (fifth stage)
  • [Figure A.8: IDoc Status Record Details] — details behind the 52 status record; status record has header section + details section
  • Header section fields:
  • IDoc number: unique number of an IDoc (951013 = inbound IDoc at example client)
  • Direction: direction of IDoc (inbound → value 2)
  • Status: status of IDoc (52 = sales order created successfully)
  • Message: adds context to Status field (message: sales order 65358 created successfully)
  • Details section — three tabs:
  • Techn.Info: time of log entry and time of database change
  • Sts details fields:
  • Code: system of origin of status messages (SAP at example client)
  • Message number: message number of a status record
  • Message type: five standard types — A (Cancel), W (Warning), E (Error), S (Success message), I (Information)
  • ID: message class ID (ZFINPRJT3 = custom message class at example client storing status messages)
  • Text: text for a status code; extracted from table T100 or connecting EDI system; & = dynamic variable pulling values from parameter fields (up to four &s from 1st–4th parameters, in order)
  • 1st parameter: stores runtime data (client stores "Sales Order" for sales order IDoc)
  • 2nd parameter: stores runtime data (client stores newly generated sales order number)
  • 3rd parameter: stores runtime data (not yet used at client)
  • 4th parameter: stores runtime data (not yet used at client)
  • Logging: message info about user name, program, subroutine
